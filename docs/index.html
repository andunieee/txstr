txstr

a decentralised, minimalist microblogging network for hackers.

install quickstart commands config etiquette hacking under the hood source

So you want to get some thoughts out into the world while also following the gibberish of a few people you actually find interesting? Instead of signing up for yet another closed platform with an algorithm deciding what you see, txstr gives you a nick, a keypair and a terminal.

Your keypair is your identity, your account. Nobody hands it to you and nobody can take it away. Your timeline is built from exactly the people you follow. There's no ranking, no likes, no reposts, no ads and no "who to follow". You find people the old way: someone hands you their key.

install

$ cargo +nightly install --locked --git https://github.com/andunieee/txstr

You'll need a nightly Rust toolchain. Don't drop --locked: it makes cargo build against the exact dependency versions txstr ships with. Building from a clone works too; the repository pins nightly in rust-toolchain.toml.

quickstart

$ txstr quickstart
txstr - quickstart
==================

this wizard will set up txstr for you.

➤ please enter your desired nick [you]: ghost
➤ paste an existing nsec, or leave empty to generate a fresh key:
➤ relays to talk to, space separated [wss://relay.damus.io wss://nos.lol wss://relay.primal.net]:
➤ publish your follow list too, so other clients can see it? (y/N):

✓ created config file at ~/.config/txstr/config.toml
✓ you are ghost — tell your friends:
  txstr follow ghost npub1…

That last line is your calling card. Paste it in a chat, put it in your .plan, print it on a sticker.

commands

txstr tweet [text…]

Posts a note. Aliased as post. With no arguments it reads from stdin, so it composes with anything:

$ txstr tweet "compiling, brb"
$ fortune -s | txstr tweet
$ uptime | txstr post

If the note is longer than character_warning, txstr asks before sending it. Be brief.

txstr timeline [-l N] [-a]

Shows the latest notes from everyone you follow, plus your own, newest first. -l sets how many, -a flips the order to oldest first.

$ txstr timeline -l 2
➤ ken (40 minutes ago):
my homelab uptime is longer than most of my relationships

➤ ada (2 hours ago):
@ken the man page agrees with you

txstr view <nick|npub> [-l N] [-a]

Same as timeline, but for a single person. Works for people you don't follow, too: just pass their key.

txstr follow <nick> <npub>

Adds someone to your follow list under a nick of your choosing. Nicks are local and personal: you can call people whatever helps you remember them.

txstr unfollow <nick>

Removes them. No notification is sent. Nobody's feelings get hurt.

txstr following

Lists everyone you follow, one per line. Pipe it, diff it, share it.

txstr whoami

Prints your nick and your public key.

mentions

Writing @nick for someone you follow turns into a real mention that their client will notice. When you read notes, mentions of people you know show up as @nick again, and everyone else is shown as a short key.

global flags

-c, --config <path> uses a different config file, handy for running several identities or a throwaway test setup.

config

Everything lives in ~/.config/txstr/config.toml. It's meant to be read and edited by hand. It also holds your secret key, so txstr writes it with 0600 permissions; keep it that way and keep it out of public dotfiles.

nick = "ghost"
secret_key = "nsec1…"
relays = ["wss://relay.damus.io", "wss://nos.lol", "wss://relay.primal.net"]
publish_follows = false
limit_timeline = 20
character_warning = 280
timeout = 10

[following]
ada = "npub1…"
ken = "npub1…"
nick
What you call yourself, and how your own notes show up in your timeline.
secret_key
Your identity. Lose it and you're starting over; leak it and someone else is you.
relays
The servers txstr talks to. Notes go to all of them and are read from all of them, so any one of them going away doesn't matter much. Run your own if you like.
publish_follows
Off by default: your follow list stays in this file. Turn it on and every follow/unfollow also publishes the list, with your nicks attached, so other clients can see it. If you already have a published list from another client, txstr merges into it instead of replacing it.
limit_timeline
How many notes timeline and view show by default.
character_warning
Ask before posting anything longer than this. Remove the line to never ask.
timeout
Seconds to wait on slow servers before moving on.
[following]
nick = key, one per line, in whatever order you like.

etiquette

  • Say things worth reading. Nobody is grading you on frequency.
  • Share your txstr following with friends. That's how people find each other here.
  • Text is the medium. If it needs a screenshot, it probably needs a blog post.
  • Be kind. It's a small place.

hacking

The repository ships a tiny in-memory server for trying things without talking to anyone:

$ cargo run --example devrelay   # ws://127.0.0.1:7777
$ txstr -c dev.toml quickstart     # and give it ws://127.0.0.1:7777 as the relay

The demo on this page is recorded against it with docs/demo/record.sh. Patches welcome.

under the hood

txstr speaks nostr, through the ritualistic library. Notes are plain kind 1 events, mentions are NIP-27 references with p tags, and a published follow list is a NIP-02 kind 3 event with your nicks as petnames. Which means your notes are readable from any other client, and anyone there is one txstr follow away.
